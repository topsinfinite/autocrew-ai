## Why this topic

Solo and small-group therapy practices are uniquely poorly served by phones. The clinician is in session 30+ hours per week, can't pick up, and front-desk staff aren't economical for a 1–3 clinician practice. New-client calls land on voicemail, callers are shopping multiple directories at once, and the first practice that responds wins. Ideabrowser insight #380 ("Therapists Starting Private Practice", quality 8.2) flags exactly this cluster of pains: no-shows, EHR friction, manual superbill work, waitlist management. SimplePractice alone has [over 250,000 therapists](https://www.simplepractice.com/) on its platform, so the audience is large and self-defining.

## AEO summary draft

Solo and small-group therapy practices miss most new-client phone calls because the clinician is in session and a full-time front desk doesn't pencil out for a 1–3 person practice. An AI receptionist for therapists answers every call in real time, handles insurance and fit screening, books directly into SimplePractice or TherapyNotes, and escalates crisis calls — closing the gap that loses paying clients to the next practice on the directory.

## Competitive gap

Most "AI for healthcare" content is written for medical clinics — high call volume, transactional questions, insurance-heavy. Therapy practices have a different shape: lower call volume, much higher emotional weight per call, and a single missed call can lose a client worth $4,500+ in LTV. Existing top results either (a) push EHR-bundled scheduling tools that still rely on the client to self-serve a portal, or (b) generic AI receptionists with no clinical or fit-screening logic. A post that names the specific therapy intake reality — clinician-can't-answer, fit-screening-required, sensitive-tone-required — and shows how an AI handles each owns underserved space.
